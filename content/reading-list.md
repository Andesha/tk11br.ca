This is where I keep track of people and pieces I want to return to. Some are useful references. Others are here because I enjoyed them and think more people should read them.

## People worth following

### [Sean Goedecke](https://www.seangoedecke.com/)

Sean writes about software engineering, AI, and how work gets done inside large technology companies. I keep an eye on his site because he has a knack for explaining the parts of engineering work that usually go unwritten.

### [Armin Ronacher](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/)

Armin writes about programming tools and the way AI is changing software development. His recent work on [Pi](https://github.com/badlogic/pi-mono) is especially interesting to me because it treats coding agents as tools people can adapt rather than fixed products.

### [Theo Browne](https://t3.gg/)

Theo talks about web development, developer tools, and the business around software. I don't agree with every take, but he pays attention to new tools and is willing to say exactly what he thinks about them.

### [Mario Zechner](https://mariozechner.at/)

Mario created [Pi](https://pi.dev/), the coding agent that Armin now works on through Earendil. I follow his work because Pi is small enough to understand and flexible enough to change, which is a much more interesting direction than another fixed coding assistant.

## Favourite pieces

Something belongs here when I can explain why I'd send someone else to it, not just because I happened to read it.

### [What I want to tell you about orbs](https://ampcode.com/notes/what-i-want-to-tell-you-about-orbs)

Thorsten Ball, August 4, 2026. Added August 21, 2026.

A very interesting post about what agents can do when you give them a strict goal. "Prove it" is something I try more often now because of this piece.

### [Linus Torvalds reaffirms that Linux is not "anti-AI" and not a "social warrior" project](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-Is-Not-Anti-AI)

Michael Larabel, July 15, 2026. Added August 21, 2026.

It's always interesting to hear from the man himself about modern software practices. Linus' trademark anger, or maybe snark, is a classic.

### [The just-say-no engineer was a ZIRP phenomenon](https://www.seangoedecke.com/the-just-say-no-engineer-was-a-zirp-phenomenon/)

Sean Goedecke, May 18, 2026. Added August 21, 2026.

This article was a big one for me. It spoke to a lot of my ongoing experience at work and in life. Worth reading from beginning to end.
